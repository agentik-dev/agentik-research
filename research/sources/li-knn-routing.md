## What it says
- The abstract reports that a well-tuned k-nearest-neighbours router "not only matches but often outperforms" state-of-the-art learned routers across tasks (instruction following, question answering, reasoning), and explains it by model performance being local in embedding space, which needs fewer samples than parametric routers. It concludes that simple baselines should be tested before complex solutions.

## Limits
- I read only the abstract page, so no experimental numbers are recorded; the word "often" means kNN does not win everywhere. Single author, preprint.
