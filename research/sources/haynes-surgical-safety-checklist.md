## What it says
- Source opened: the Europe PMC record of the abstract (the address above), plus the AHRQ Patient Safety Network page and the Harvard repository page for the same paper. The NEJM page returned HTTP 403.
- Design: eight hospitals in eight cities, in a range of economic settings, took part from October 2007 to September 2008. Researchers collected data prospectively on 3,733 consecutive patients aged 16 or older having noncardiac surgery, then on 3,955 consecutive patients after a 19-item Surgical Safety Checklist was introduced. The primary end point was complications, including death, in hospital within 30 days.
- Death rate: 1.5% before the checklist and 0.8% after (P=0.003).
- Inpatient complications: 11.0% of patients at baseline and 7.0% after (P<0.001).
- The checklist is short and fixed: three pauses (sign in before anaesthesia, time out before incision, sign out before the patient leaves) with 19 items in all, built from WHO's Safe Surgery Saves Lives guidelines.
- The abstract says the introduction was "associated with" the reductions, and it cites an estimated 234 million operations each year as the context.

## Limits
- A before-and-after comparison of two consecutive cohorts at the same sites, with no concurrent control group and no randomisation. The abstract claims association, not cause.
- The checklist was introduced with a programme and teamwork measures, so the gain cannot be assigned to the list alone.
- I have not opened any later study of checklists, so I do not know whether the result replicated.
- It is a protocol read at each operation, not a log of decisions. It supports "a short written list, read every time, changes outcomes"; it does not support any particular length.
