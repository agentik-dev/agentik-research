## What it says
- The original National Patient Safety Goal called for the complete and immediate elimination of many common abbreviations; pharmacists were to call prescribers about banned abbreviations and, except in emergencies, reject those orders.
- The accreditor then backed off, and now aims to restrict abbreviations in handwritten, preprinted or free-text entries in patient care and medication records.
- The article names QD (daily) and U (units) as examples. It says "q.d." or "QD" is the most common problem because it can be mistaken for "q.i.d." (four times a day).
- It says the prohibited list draws largely on the case studies and recommendations of the Institute for Safe Medication Practices, which had compiled more than 60 abbreviations, symbols and dose designations linked to medication errors.

## Limits
- A trade-press report from 2005, and it gives no deadlines or compliance dates. It is evidence that a ban on shorthand was first drawn wide and then narrowed to a short list with a defined scope, not evidence of the ban's effect on error rates.
- The page text I read did not give an author.
