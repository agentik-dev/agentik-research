## What it says
I read the journal's abstract as the Europe PMC record prints it. The NEJM page returned HTTP 403 and the article is not in PubMed Central, so I did not read the full text.
- Design: a prospective intervention study of a resident handoff-improvement program in nine hospitals, comparing a preintervention period with a postintervention period. Error rates came from active surveillance. Handoffs were scored from printed handoff documents and audio recordings. Resident workflow was scored from time-motion observation. The primary outcome had two parts: medical errors and preventable adverse events.
- Headline result, over 10,740 patient admissions: medical errors fell 23 percent, from 24.5 to 18.8 per 100 admissions (P<0.001). Preventable adverse events fell 30 percent, from 4.7 to 3.3 per 100 admissions (P<0.001). Nonpreventable adverse events did not change significantly (3.0 and 2.8 per 100 admissions, P=0.79).
- Site level: significant error reductions at six of the nine sites.
- Handoff content: every one of 14 prespecified key elements (nine written, five oral) appeared more often after the intervention (P<0.001 for all 14 comparisons).
- Cost to workflow: none found. Oral handoffs lasted 2.4 minutes per patient before and 2.5 after (P=0.55), and patient-family contact time and computer time did not change significantly.
- What the intervention held: a mnemonic to standardize oral and written handoffs, handoff and communication training, a faculty development and observation program, and a sustainability campaign. The mnemonic's five parts are in starmer-ipass-mnemonic-pediatrics-2012.md.
- Funding line in the abstract: the Office of the Assistant Secretary for Planning and Evaluation of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, and others.
- The authors' conclusion: the program was associated with fewer medical errors and fewer preventable adverse events and with better communication, without a negative effect on workflow.

## Limits
- Abstract only. The abstract does not give the length of the training or the wording of the 14 elements, and I did not confirm either.
- A before-and-after comparison; the abstract names no concurrent control group. "Associated with" is the authors' own wording.
- Residents in nine hospitals; a hospital-ward handoff between people, not between model sessions. The pediatric setting comes from hospitalist-ipass-handoff-reduced-errors.md, a news page.
