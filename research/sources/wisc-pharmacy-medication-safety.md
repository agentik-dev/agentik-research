## What it says
- A short timeline of the Joint Commission's "Do Not Use" list. In 2001 the Joint Commission issued a Sentinel Event Alert on medical abbreviations. In 2002 its Board of Commissioners approved a National Patient Safety Goal that required accredited organizations to build their own list of abbreviations not to use. In 2004 the Joint Commission created its "Do Not Use" list to meet that goal.
- In 2010 the goal (NPSG.02.02.01) was folded into the Information Management standards as elements of performance 2 and 3 under IM.02.02.01.
- The page does not reproduce the list; it links to the Joint Commission's page and to the Institute for Safe Medication Practices' list.

## Limits
- A school's policy page, not the Joint Commission. It gives years but no exact effective date. I could not find, in any page I opened, a primary statement of the day the list took effect.
- Other pages I opened agree on 2004 but are also secondary: a 2026 CASRAI guide says the list "began as a National Patient Safety Goal in 2004" (casrai-joint-commission-do-not-use-list), and a 2005 trade article describes the requirement being softened (drugtopics-jcaho-reconsiders-abbreviation-protocols).
