## What it says
- A list of the published sources behind each design concept, "fetched and read during the research runs (2026-08-31)", in seven groups: why swarms drift into shorthand (Clark and Wilkes-Gibbs; Clark and Brennan; Pickering and Garrod; Garrod et al.; Zipf's brevity law; Piantadosi, Tily and Gibson, PNAS 2011); governed shorthand in the wild (tactical brevity codes, Q codes, medical abbreviation bans); machine-lane prior art (Lewis et al. 2017 on negotiation agents, arXiv 1706.05125; the Agora protocol, arXiv 2410.11905; LLMLingua and LLMLingua-2; TOON; SynthLang; DroidSpeak, arXiv 2411.02820; GibberLink; Caveman); the AI-tell corpus (Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing"; Kobak et al., arXiv 2406.07016; the AntiSlop sampler); style as data (Vale, Mailchimp, Google, Diataxis); communication standards (plainlanguage.gov, GOV.UK, ASD-STE100, BLUF, Minto, Radical Candor, Crucial Conversations); and the substrate (three Anthropic engineering posts and the Claude Code docs).
- Each line gives one sentence on the design concept the source supports and a URL. The full set of URLs is listed in `research/synthesis/tools-respeak.md` section 5.

## Limits
- Three of its Wikipedia links (conceptual pact, interactive alignment, experimental semiotics) are recorded as returning 404 in respeak's own linguistics note (see `agentik-respeak-research-survey`), so the list carries links its own research found dead.
- Several entries are Wikipedia pages standing in for primary papers.
