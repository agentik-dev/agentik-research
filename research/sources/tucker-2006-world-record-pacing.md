The abstract was opened on a Europe PMC record; WebFetch returned a paraphrase, so no verbatim quote is taken.

## What it says
- Method: split-time analysis of men's world records. 26 records at 800 m (1912 to 1997), 32 at 5000 m (1922 to 2004) and 34 at 10,000 m (1921 to 2004). The 800 m records were analyzed by lap and the two longer events by kilometre.
- 800 m: the second lap was significantly slower than the first, and only 2 of the 26 records had a faster second lap.
- 5000 m and 10,000 m: the first and final kilometres were significantly faster than the middle kilometres, an overall even pace with an end spurt.
- Authors' reading: the best pacing differs by event. In the 800 m runners go fastest early and cannot speed up much later; in the two longer events they hold back a reserve in the middle and spend it at the end. They frame pacing as a regulated balance between the demands of performance and the body's homeostasis.

## Limits
- A descriptive analysis of record runs by elite men on a track, from the abstract only. Records are the best outcomes, not typical races, and no comparison group of failed pacing is in the abstract. Events are 800 m to 10,000 m on a track and the abstract gives no durations, so transfer to hours-long efforts is not tested.
- The abstract prints two lap-time means for the 800 m with an unclear lap order; they are left out here.
