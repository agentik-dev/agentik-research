## What it says
- Index page: says the Plain Writing Act of 2010 "established the requirement that content for the public is written for its specific audience." It lists four guides: principles of plain language, writing for understanding, design for understanding, test for understanding.
- Principles page: write for your audience first, and do not "dumb down" content for everyone; open with a topic sentence; use active voice; organize by previewing what is coming and summarizing lengthy documents up front; use tables and lists for detail; test content. It cites research that the user experience of federal communications is lacking and does not state a definition of plain language.
- Writing page: active voice, present tense, strongest direct verb form, shorter words and short sections. It gives no word counts, no one-idea-per-sentence rule and no main-point-first rule.
- Test page: start early and test at least twice; it names paraphrase testing, usability testing and controlled comparative studies, and says focus groups suit learning about an audience before writing. It does not mention readability formulas or scores.

## Limits
- Guidance pages without a method or numbers. As of October 2026 this guide is where the old plainlanguage.gov addresses redirect.
- Sentence and paragraph rules are on the separate guideline pages (see gsa-federal-plain-language-guidelines), not here.
