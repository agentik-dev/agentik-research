## What it says
- Sample: 360 students in grades 2 to 5 each read six written passages aloud, and the researchers scored oral reading fluency.
- Eight readability formulas rated the passages. They disagreed widely: one passage was rated anywhere from first-grade to fifth-grade level, depending on the formula.
- Compared with the students' actual performance, seven of the eight formulas were less than 49 percent accurate, the worst was 17 percent accurate, and the best was 79 percent accurate.
- The lead author advised caution about using readability levels to assign reading, and the abstract urges assessing text difficulty by more than a formula.
- A second news summary I opened (ScienceDaily) reports the same numbers.

## Limits
- I read the university news release and the ScienceDaily summary, not the paper. The Reading Rockets page for the paper returned HTTP 403.
- Children's oral reading fluency, not adults reading technical prose. It shows that formula grade levels can disagree with each other and with measured performance. It does not show how they behave on engineering text.
