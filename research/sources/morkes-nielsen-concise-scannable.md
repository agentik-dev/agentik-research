## What it says
- Three studies with 81 users in total. The percentages come from the third, a measurement study: 51 experienced Web users tested five versions of a travel site that held the same information in different writing styles: promotional (the control), scannable, concise, objective, and a combined version.
- Tasks: two fact-finding searches, one judgment task, a 10-minute study period followed by a memory exam, and drawing the site's structure from memory.
- Overall usability score against the promotional control: concise 58 percent better, scannable 47 percent better, objective 27 percent better, combined 124 percent better.
- The inverted pyramid (conclusion first, then detail) is mentioned three times: users in the first study liked summaries and that style; a news story written that way drew positive comments in the second; and the concise version was partly written that way. It was never tested as a separate condition.

## Limits
- 1997 web pages read by experienced users on a travel site, not reports read by engineers.
- Each version differs in several ways at once, so the figures cannot be credited to conclusion-first order alone.
