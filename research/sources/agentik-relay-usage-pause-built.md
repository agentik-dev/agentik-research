## What it says
- There is no band pacing design in this repo. A case-insensitive whole-word search for `band`, `bands`, `pacing`, `pace` and `paced` finds one unrelated hit (an icon's "grip bands", `history/sittings/2026-09-23-17.md:13`). Git history is one squashed commit, so no earlier design was lost to a search of history. What the repo has is a reactive pause: the night keeps running until the account refuses, then waits for the reset.
- The owner's request that started it (`design/usage-limit-resume-r1.md:3`, quoted at `design/packets/ar65-limit-pause.md:40`): "sessions and relay runs resume by themselves after a usage limit (the 5-hour window), instead of stopping." The design is dated 2026-10-10 and says it was written before code.
- The numbers, design (`usage-limit-resume-r1.md`) and code (`pause.go`): wake at the reset plus 2 minutes (`WakeSlack`, `pause.go:16`); a limit with no reset waits 30 minutes (`FallbackWait`, `:17`), and the wake's sitting is the probe; with no `--until`, a wake over 6 hours away stops the night (`PauseReach`, `:18`); at most 12 pauses a night (`MaxPauses`, `:23`); a reset past 8 days reads as no reset (design line 32); a continuation gets the row's turns and dollars less what earlier parts spent, never under 10 turns or $5 (line 43).
- Limit kinds (design lines 30, 34; `pauseFor`, `pause.go:85-121`): the five-hour window (`five_hour`, text "session") pauses the whole night; the weekly limit (`seven_day`) stops the night at once; a limit on one named model pauses only that model's rows when it names a reset, and stops the night when it names none; an unknown limit waits once, 30 minutes. A stop also comes while draining, after 12 pauses, when the wake is at or past `--until`, and when the same reset is returned twice.
- What the pause keeps: each paused row keeps its worktree and stage lock; the relay and its `relay.pid` stay held through the pause (design lines 51, 87), which refuses the owner's own `gate`, `plan`, `run` and `resume` for up to 6 hours or to `--until`. The continuation is a fresh session with a RESUME block, not `claude --resume`, because the cache is cold and one row sat at 206k context (design line 96).
- Evidence the design rests on (design lines 7 to 17): five weekly or model-limit stops in the relay's own ledger (two weekly; three on one named model with no reset), none of them a five-hour window; and 96 CLI transcript files (CLI 2.1.263 to 2.1.296) holding 35 session-limit, 35 weekly-limit and 46 model-limit messages, grepped on 2026-10-10. The 46 model-limit messages named no reset, and one such limit outlasted 9 h 43 m. Line 17 says the owner's own case, the five-hour window, has 35 hits in transcripts and none in the relay's ledger. Design line 65 says no probe ran and that the evidence is section 1.

The one recorded real event, `internal/stream/testdata/real.jsonl:8`, is a `rate_limit_event` with status `allowed_warning` and a `unifiedWindows` object: `five_hour` utilization 0.25 and `seven_day` utilization 0.33, each with its own `resetsAt`. So the pause acts on a refusal, never on how full the window is. Nothing in the repo spaces a night's work across a window or a weekly reset ahead of a limit; the only spacing is `SpawnSpacing`, 30 seconds between spawns under load (`history/sittings/2026-09-23-13.md:31`), which is a load control.

## Limits
- Every test of the pause runs on a fake spawner and a stepped clock (design line 65). The repo records no live night that paused and woke. So the pause is designed and unit-tested, with no field measurement of a wake, of the 2-minute margin, or of how often a night meets a window.
- The transcript counts come from one machine's local CLI transcripts, one account, one time zone, on one day, and the design says the stream's fields are the CLI's and unpublished (line 89). A sleeping laptop wakes a night late, never early (line 88).
- The five ledger stops are inferred to be event-named: the relay keeps no raw stream (line 7).
- Model names, row names and the owner's time zone are not repeated in this note.
