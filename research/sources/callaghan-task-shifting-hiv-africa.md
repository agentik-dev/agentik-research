## What it says
- Design: a systematic literature review. It searched Medline, the Cochrane library, the Social Science Citation Index and a South African national research database, plus bibliographies, author contacts, grey literature and conference abstracts.
- Counts: 2,960 articles found, 84 included in the core review, 51 of those reporting outcomes, from 10 sub-Saharan African countries.
- The most common intervention: delegating tasks, especially starting and monitoring antiretroviral therapy, from doctors to nurses and other non-physician clinicians.
- Outcomes: 5 studies showed increased access to therapy through expanded clinical capacity. 2 concluded task shifting is cost effective. 9 showed staff giving equal or better quality of care. Studies on whether non-physician clinicians agreed with physician decisions were mixed, and most showed good agreement.
- Conclusion: the authors judge task shifting an effective way to address the workforce shortage, giving high-quality, cost-effective care to more patients than a physician-centred model. They name the conditions: adequate and sustainable training, support and pay for staff in new roles, integration into teams, and regulatory compliance. They advise "careful implementation".
- It defines task shifting as delegating tasks that physicians perform to staff with lower-level qualifications, and it credits the 2008 WHO guidelines (see `who-task-shifting-2008`) with formalising the approach.

## Limits
- The counts are study counts, not effect sizes. "Equal or better quality" rests on 9 studies, and the review pools no common outcome measure in the abstract I read.
- Setting: HIV care in sub-Saharan Africa, where physicians are scarce. The result may not carry to settings where the expert is not the constraint.
- A review of observational and some trial evidence; the abstract gives no risk-of-bias rating. The reference list inconsistently dates the WHO guidelines (2008 and 2007).
- Read through the PubMed Central page and an abstract record from Europe PMC; I did not read the full body text.
