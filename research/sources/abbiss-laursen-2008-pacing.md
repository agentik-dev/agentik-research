## What it says
- A review of how athletes spread effort and energy across an event. Models built on power and velocity or force and time identify six patterns: negative, all-out, positive, even, parabolic and variable.
- By duration: events of 30 seconds or less may suit an all-out start; in events longer than 2 minutes, performance may improve with a more even pace.
- Evidence is thin for middle-distance events (1.5 to 2 minutes) and ultra-endurance events (over 4 hours). In those, well-trained athletes tend to use a positive pattern, slowing progressively after peak speed.
- Mechanism, stated as a proposal: the brain sets self-selected intensity by weighing sensory feedback from the body against the work still to do, and the rate and capacity limits of aerobic and anaerobic energy supply strongly shape the best pace.

## Limits
- A review, so it reports patterns across studies and gives no new measurement; the abstract carries no effect sizes. Only the abstract was opened, not the full text. Published in 2008, before most of the later pacing work.
- The ultra-endurance claim is about events over 4 hours of continuous exertion by athletes; whether an agent's usage window behaves the same is our reading, not the paper's.
