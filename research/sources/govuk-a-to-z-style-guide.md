## What it says
- Plain English entry: "All content on GOV.UK should be written in plain English." The entry on words to avoid says plain English is mandatory and lists words such as "utilise" (use "use") and "leverage" (use "influence" or "use").
- Sentence length entry: "Check sentences with more than 25 words to see if you can split them to make them clearer." The threshold is 25 words, and it is a prompt to check, not a hard cap.
- Metaphors are to be avoided because they "lead to slower comprehension". Negative contractions such as "can't" are to be avoided because many users find them harder to read. Active voice is preferred for concise, clear content.
- Jargon: technical terms may be used where needed ("They're not jargon") and are explained on first use. An abbreviation is spelled out in full the first time it appears on a page.
- Front-loading: link text and page titles lead with the relevant terms; page titles are limited to 65 characters. Summaries are limited to 160 characters.
- The page has no entry on readability scores, reading age, bottom line or inverted pyramid.

## Limits
- A house style guide for one government site. It cites no studies for its reasons (slower comprehension, harder to read).
- I did not find a reading-age figure on any GOV.UK page I opened. Several local councils attribute a reading age of 9 to GOV.UK guidance; I could not confirm that from GOV.UK itself.
- The older address (gov.uk/guidance/style-guide) redirects to this host as of October 2026.
