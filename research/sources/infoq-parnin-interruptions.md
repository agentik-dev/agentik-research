## What it says
A secondary news report; the primary study behind its figures is not the paper I opened (see Limits).
- Re-opened 2026-10-14; the article text matches the earlier lines below. It names the research as Chris Parnin's, published under the name "ninlabs research", from his article "Programmer Interrupted" and a related paper.
- It summarises programmer-interruption research: 10,000 recorded programming sessions from 86 programmers using Eclipse and Visual Studio.
- Programmers took 10 to 15 minutes to resume editing code after an interruption; when interrupted in the middle of editing a method, they resumed within a minute only 10 percent of the time; a programmer was likely to get only one uninterrupted 2-hour session a day.

## Limits
- Secondary source. A related primary paper by Parnin and Rugaber is now noted in parnin-rugaber-resumption-strategies.md. It reports 85 programmers and 10 percent of all sessions with a first edit inside a minute; it does not report the 10 to 15 minute figure as its own result, and its 35 percent figure for resuming within a minute applies to sessions that finish the last-edited method. I have not shown that the article and that paper describe the same analysis, so the article's framing may differ from the paper's. Research reported in 2013, before coding agents; an agent's question is a different kind of interruption from an editing-time one.
