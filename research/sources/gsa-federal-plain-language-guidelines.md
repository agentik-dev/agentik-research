## What it says
- Index page: these are the writing guidelines for the Plain Writing Act of 2010. They run in this order: audience, organization, writing principles (the longest section, word level up to paragraphs and sections), document and web writing, testing techniques. They first targeted regulations and later widened.
- Organize section (`organize/index.md`): "Start by stating your purpose and the bottom line." "Put the most important information at the beginning and include background information (when necessary) toward the end." This is the federal plain-language form of bottom line up front.
- Page "Write short sentences" (`concise/write-short-sentences.md`): gives no word limit. Its rule is one idea per sentence, and it says long, complicated sentences often signal that the writer is unsure what to say. Splitting an "if" clause into its own sentence is its worked example. It cites plain-language and legal-writing sources (Charrow, Erhardt and Charrow 2007; Garner 2001; Kimble 2005 and 2006; Murawski 1999; Office of the Federal Register 1998; Redish 1991; the SEC Plain English Handbook 1998) and supports the advice with before-and-after rewrites, not measured data.
- Page "Write short paragraphs" (`concise/write-short-paragraphs.md`): paragraphs of no more than 150 words in three to eight sentences, with a hard ceiling of 250 words; one subject per paragraph, opened by a topic sentence; an occasional one-sentence paragraph is allowed.
- The Concise directory also lists pages on keeping subject, verb and object close together, positive language, and short sections (listed, not read).

## Limits
- Expert consensus plus worked examples. The pages I read cite no experiment and give no effect size.
- I read the Markdown source in the public repository that feeds plainlanguage.gov. As of October 2026 the website's own guideline URLs redirect to a Digital.gov guide, so the rendered site text was not read.
- The federal guidelines are written for documents aimed at the public, not for engineers reading agent reports.
