## What it says
- A Y-statement records a decision and its justification in one sentence with six parts: the context (a requirement or component), "facing" a desired quality, the decision ("we decided for"), the alternatives rejected ("and neglected"), the benefit ("to achieve") and the accepted drawback ("accepting that").
- The page positions it as a lean alternative to longer decision records, and says the template can be adapted, for example by adding a "because" clause or splitting a long sentence. The name is the Y-shaped sentence, which sounds like "why".

## Limits
- A practitioner's description of a template, with no study of how Y-statements are read or kept current. I read only the section on Y-statements.
