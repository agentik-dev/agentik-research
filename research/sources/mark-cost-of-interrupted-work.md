## What it says
I read the PDF text.
- Method: 48 participants (81 percent German university students, mean age 26) answered work emails in a lab while a "supervisor" interrupted them by telephone or instant message every two minutes, with questions in the same context as the task, in a different context, or none (baseline).
- Results (abstract): the context of the interruption did not change the cost; people finished interrupted tasks in less time with no difference in quality, and the price was more stress, frustration, time pressure and effort. Openness to experience and need for personal structure predicted how costly interruptions were.

## Limits
- A 90-minute lab session with student participants and email tasks, a research note of four pages. It measures work with interruptions against work without them, not batching against piecemeal questions, and not software work.
