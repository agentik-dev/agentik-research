## What it says
- Four Afrikaans formulas (two by Van Rooyen 1986, one by McDermid Heyns 2007, one by McKellar 2008) were tested on ten texts: five magazine articles and five government communication documents. Five cloze tests were built per text, giving 50 tests; 149 speakers took two cloze tests each.
- Both Van Rooyen formulas were not significantly correlated with cloze scores. The McKellar formula was. The McDermid Heyns formula came close to significance. The McKellar and McDermid Heyns formulas together accounted for about 40 percent of the variance in cloze scores.
- Average characters per word alone predicted about 65 percent of the variance in cloze scores, which the authors call considerably better than the formulas.

## Limits
- Afrikaans formulas, not the English Flesch-Kincaid or Gunning-Fog, and only ten texts. The abstract gives no correlation coefficients or p-values.
- It shows that a surface feature can beat a published formula on a comprehension test. It does not test English technical prose.
