## What it says
- Diátaxis is a framework for planning and writing technical documentation. It names four needs and four matching forms: tutorials, how-to guides, reference, explanation.
- The map (start-here page) has two axes: action versus cognition, and acquisition of skill (study) versus application of skill (work). Tutorials are action plus acquisition: a lesson that builds skill and confidence, not one that finishes a task. How-to guides are action plus application: directions for a competent user with a real goal. Reference is cognition plus application: accurate, neutral facts whose structure mirrors the thing described. Explanation is cognition plus acquisition: context that answers "why".
- Foundations page: the framework rests on a "systematic description and analysis of generalised user needs" (learning, goals, information, understanding). It argues the four forms cover the whole map: "There is simply no other territory to cover."
- Theory page: the aim is to put documentation practice on a firmer theoretical footing than rules of thumb.
- Start page, on its own status: it calls the approach pragmatic, and the author writes "I think it's true, but what matters is that it actually helps people create better documentation."
- Application and how-to-use pages: the framework is "a guide, not a plan"; take small steps (pick a piece of documentation, assess it, decide one action, do it, repeat) instead of planning the whole structure first. The pages say nothing about content that fits none of the four forms.

## Limits
- The claim rests on an argued analysis of user needs. The pages I read cite no study of reader outcomes.
- It is written for product documentation. Whether it fits a research site or agent reports is our inference, not something the pages say.
- The pages show no publication dates.
