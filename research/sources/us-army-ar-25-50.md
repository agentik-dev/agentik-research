## What it says
- Paragraph 1-11 (Writing quality), citing the Plain Writing Act of 2010: Army writing "will be clear, concise, and well-organized", and the reader must be able to understand the writer's ideas in a single reading.
- Paragraph 1-38b (Standards for Army writing): "Two essential requirements include putting the main point at the beginning of the correspondence (bottom line up front) and using the active voice". This is the only passage in the text that uses the phrase.
- Paragraph 1-39b (plain language techniques): use short words; keep the average sentence at about 15 words; paragraphs of no more than 10 lines with few exceptions; avoid jargon; avoid sentences that begin "It is", "There is" or "There are"; write one-page letters and memorandums for most correspondence and put extra detail in enclosures.
- Paragraph 2-4b(1) (memorandum body): begin with a short, clear purpose sentence, then put the recommendation, conclusion or most important information (the main point) next. The subject line is 10 words or fewer where possible and avoids abbreviations.
- Paragraph 2-8 (decision memorandum): not more than two pages excluding supporting documents; body holds purpose, recommendations, discussion and coordination.
- Paragraph 1-15 (abbreviations, brevity codes, acronyms): memorandums may use abbreviations and brevity codes authorized on the Army Publishing Directorate site. For letters to persons outside the Department of Defense, "Do not use military abbreviations, brevity codes, acronyms, or military jargon". Use only well-known abbreviations or those you believe the recipient knows; define a lengthy term once, then use the shortened form only if it will be repeated.

## Limits
- A regulation states a standard. It gives no evidence that bottom line up front or a 15-word average helps a reader act.
- The fetch tool returned the PDF as compressed data, so I extracted the text locally with pdftotext and read the paragraphs above from that output.
- The cited copy is hosted by a state government's emergency management office, not by the Army Publishing Directorate. Its summary of change shows later administrative revisions; the paragraph numbers above are from that copy.
