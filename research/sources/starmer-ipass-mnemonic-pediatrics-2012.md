## What it says
I opened the PubMed Central page; the fetch tool returned its text, not its figures.
- The mnemonic has five parts: Illness severity, Patient summary, Action list, Situation awareness and contingency planning, and Synthesis by receiver.
- Design: the authors modeled the process on how the SBAR handoff mnemonic was designed. They brainstormed the essential elements of a verbal handoff and focused on the elements that faculty observers most often saw missing from resident handoffs: illness severity assessment, contingency planning, and read-back by the receiving resident. They also drew on best practices in the literature.
- A pilot of an earlier format, SIGNOUT, showed that most handoffs did not follow its structure. Chief residents asked for a mnemonic that was shorter, easier to remember, and made of distinct elements with no overlap. The elements also had to fit into computerized handoff tools.
- The patient summary can be adapted to hold information specific to each type of provider.

## Limits
- The fetched text names each element but says little about what each holds; the example handoff is in figures I could not read. A short design article, not a trial: it reports no error rates. The measured result is in starmer-ipass-handoff-nejm-2014.md.
