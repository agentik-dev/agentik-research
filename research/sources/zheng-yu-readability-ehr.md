## What it says
- Three formulas were tested against laypeople's perceived difficulty: Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level, SMOG and the Gunning-Fog Index. The corpus was 140 English Wikipedia articles on diabetes and 242 de-identified clinical notes with diabetes diagnosis codes. Fifteen crowd workers compared document pairs and rated difficulty from 1 (easiest) to 10 (most difficult).
- Correlations between individual raters and formula scores were low. For the Wikipedia articles, r was 0.1758 (Flesch-Kincaid), 0.4134 (SMOG) and 0.2695 (Gunning-Fog). For the clinical notes, r was 0.2999, 0.1024 and 0.1272 in the same order.
- Raters agreed with each other more than with the formulas: 7 of 15 averaged r above 0.6 with the other raters, 5 averaged 0.4 to 0.6, and 3 averaged below 0.4.
- The formulas scored clinical notes as easier than the Wikipedia articles (Flesch-Kincaid difference of -33.09 percent), but raters judged the notes harder by 21.31 percent. Documents of different genres with similar Flesch-Kincaid scores were still perceived as differing in difficulty (P < .001). The formulas correlated strongly with one another.
- The number of medical concepts in a document correlated better with perceived difficulty (r = 0.4434 for all concepts, 0.5041 for unique concepts, in the Wikipedia articles) than any formula did. The authors conclude the formulas "were not appropriate to assess the readability of EHR notes" and suggest metrics that count concept complexity.

## Limits
- Raters were crowd workers, not patients, and all documents concerned one condition, so prior knowledge may have shaped ratings (the authors' own limits).
- The outcome is perceived difficulty, not a comprehension test, and the text is clinical notes, not software or agent prose. It is the nearest measured test of the formulas on specialist technical text that I found.
