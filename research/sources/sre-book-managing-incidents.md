## What it says
- A separate communication role is "the public face of the incident response task force": it issues periodic updates to the response team and stakeholders, usually by email, and may keep the incident document current.
- The incident commander's most important responsibility is maintaining a living incident document that several people can edit at once, kept in a template with the most important information at the top and retained for the postmortem.
- Hand-off between incident commanders is explicit: the outgoing commander briefs the incoming one, confirms the transfer, waits for firm acknowledgment, and announces the change to everyone working the incident.
- The chapter contrasts an unmanaged incident, where nobody knew what others were doing, with a managed one in which an email thread keeps executives informed at a high level without burying them in detail and a chat channel serves as a reliable log.

## Limits
- Describes practice, with an illustrative case and no measurement. It is a precedent for a person-facing summary kept apart from the working log, for important-first layout, and for explicit hand-offs; it does not discuss shorthand.
