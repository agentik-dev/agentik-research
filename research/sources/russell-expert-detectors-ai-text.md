## What it says
- Annotators read 300 non-fiction English articles, labelled each human-written or AI-generated, and wrote paragraph-length explanations. AI text came from commercial models (GPT-4o, Claude, o1).
- Annotators who frequently use language models for writing detected AI text well without training or feedback. A majority vote of five such annotators misclassified 1 of 300 articles.
- That majority vote beat most commercial and open-source detectors, including when the text was paraphrased or "humanized".
- Their cues were specific lexical clues ("AI vocabulary") and qualities such as formality, originality and clarity.

## Limits
- Abstract page only: no accuracy for single annotators and no detector figures. The sample is articles, not engineering prose, and the annotators were heavy users of the tools.
- It shows frequent users can spot machine prose and that vocabulary is a cue. It does not show that readers dislike it or act differently.
