## What it says
- This is a one-page reproduction of The Joint Commission's official "Do Not Use" list. The Joint Commission's own fact sheet, its list page and its PDF all returned HTTP 403 to the note author's fetch tool, so the note's author could not open the official page itself.
- The list has eight rows, each with the potential problem and the replacement to write:
  - "U, u (unit)": mistaken for zero, for the number four, or for "cc"; write "unit".
  - "IU (International Unit)": mistaken for IV (intravenous) or for the number 10; write "International Unit".
  - "Q.D., QD, q.d., qd (daily)": mistaken for each other; write "daily".
  - "Q.O.D., QOD, q.o.d., qod (every other day)": the period after the Q is mistaken for "I" and the "O" for "I"; write "every other day".
  - Trailing zero (X.0 mg): the decimal point is missed; write X mg.
  - Lack of leading zero (.X mg): same problem; write 0.X mg.
  - "MS": can mean morphine sulfate or magnesium sulfate; write "morphine sulfate".
  - "MSO4" and "MgSO4": confused for one another; write "magnesium sulfate".
- Scope (footnote): it applies to all orders and all medication-related documentation that is handwritten, including free-text computer entry, or on pre-printed forms.
- Exception: a trailing zero may be used where it shows the precision of a reported value, such as laboratory results, imaging reports of lesion size, or catheter and tube sizes, but never in medication orders or medication documentation.
- The mechanism behind the rule is misreading, not ignorance: each row names a way a hurried reader takes the short form for a different one.

## Limits
- A secondary copy: a department's reproduction, with an update stamp of 2011-11-22, not the Joint Commission's current page. A later revision may differ; one source I opened (the CASRAI guide, see casrai-joint-commission-do-not-use-list) groups the same entries as six items rather than eight rows.
- The copy gives no effective year and no error counts. The year comes from other notes and is secondary.
