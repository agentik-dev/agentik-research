## What it says
- A large-scale human evaluation of several neural abstractive summarization systems found that all model summaries contained "substantial amounts of hallucinated content".
- Pretrained models did better than the others on automatic scores and on human judgments of faithfulness and factuality.
- Textual-entailment measures correlated better with faithfulness than standard metrics did.

## Limits
- Abstract page only; it gives no percentage of summaries affected and does not describe the annotation protocol.
- Pre-2021 models, so it shows that unfaithful summaries are a known class of error and says nothing about current models.
