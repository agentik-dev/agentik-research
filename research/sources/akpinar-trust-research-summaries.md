## What it says
- A mixed-methods survey experiment with three conditions: human-written, language-model-edited and language-model-written abstracts. Outcomes were judgments of quality and trustworthiness and whether readers with machine-learning expertise could identify the language-model text.
- Participants struggled to tell language-model text from human writing. Their beliefs about language-model involvement, accurate or not, significantly shaped their evaluations.
- Abstracts edited by a language model were rated more favourably than those written only by humans or only by the model. Three reader orientations toward language-model-assisted writing emerged.

## Limits
- Abstract page only: no sample size or effect sizes on the page.
- Abstracts of research papers read by readers with machine-learning expertise.
