## What it says
- This note records two listing pages for the WHO document and a peer-reviewed review that describes it. The WHO full text did not open (see Limits).
- The CHW Central page lists the author as the World Health Organization and the year as 2008. It says the document offers a "set of 22 recommendations to provide guidance to countries" that are considering adopting or extending task shifting, drawn from a one-year process of country consultation, data gathering and wide consultation with experts and stakeholders.
- The HRH Global Resource Center page (https://www.hrhresourcecenter.org/node/1811.html) lists the publisher as the World Health Organization, the year as 2007, 92 pages and 22 guidelines. It says the document proposes restructuring and spreading out health services through task shifting to address health worker shortages.
- Callaghan, Ford and Schneider (note `callaghan-task-shifting-hiv-africa`) describe the guidelines as joint WHO, UNAIDS and PEPFAR guidelines issued in 2008. They call them "an immediate way to address staff shortages while delivering good quality care", and they define task shifting as delegating tasks that physicians perform to staff with lower-level qualifications.
- Our reading, not the source's words: the mechanism is to move a defined task to a worker with less training and keep the scarce expert for what only the expert can do. The HIV review frames the guidelines as a response to staff shortages in HIV care. The two listings I opened do not state the document's scope.

## Limits
- I did not read the recommendations themselves. Several WHO repository addresses (the record, its PDF and a publication page) returned HTTP 403 or 404, and I did not work around the block. I cannot quote any recommendation's wording.
- The two listings disagree on the year (2008 versus 2007). Callaghan and colleagues' reference list gives both years for the same guidelines.
- The listing pages are secondary hosts, not WHO. The document reports no outcome figure that I have read; the measured results sit in the two review notes `callaghan-task-shifting-hiv-africa` and `laurant-cochrane-nurse-substitution`.
